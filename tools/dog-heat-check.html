Walk Heat Safety Checker ☀️

Air that feels fine to you can be dangerous to a fur coat with no sweat glands. Check before you clip the leash.

Humidity and full sun push every verdict one level worse. Always do the 7-second pavement test: back of your hand flat on the asphalt, if you can't hold 7 seconds, it burns paws. Full guide: walking in hot weather.

When is it too hot to walk a dog?

Dogs cannot sweat through their skin. They cool by panting, which stops working well above about 27°C in humid air, and flat-faced breeds (pugs, bulldogs, shih tzus), seniors, overweight dogs and thick double coats sit one row stricter on every threshold. Heatstroke can set in within minutes of exertion in the wrong conditions and is fatal in a meaningful share of cases that reach a vet.

The checker above combines air temperature, humidity and your dog's risk group into a plain answer: go, go carefully, or stay in. Pavement is the other half. Asphalt in the sun runs 20 to 30 degrees hotter than the air; if you cannot hold the back of your hand on it for seven seconds, it will burn paws. Early morning and late evening walks on grass solve most summer days.

Signs that a walk has already gone wrong: heavy panting that does not settle within a few minutes of rest, thick drool, a bright red tongue, staggering, vomiting. Move to shade, wet the belly and paws with cool (not icy) water, offer small drinks and call the vet on the way.

Common questions

What temperature is too hot to walk a dog?

Above 27°C most dogs should walk only briefly in shade; above 32°C stay in for all but flat-faced and senior dogs, who should stay in from about 24°C. Humidity lowers every threshold.

How do I check if the pavement is too hot?

Press the back of your hand to the surface for seven seconds. If you cannot, walk on grass or wait for the evening.

Can I shave my dog to keep them cool?

Usually no. Double coats insulate against heat as well as cold and protect from sunburn. Brushing out the undercoat helps; shaving to the skin often makes things worse.
