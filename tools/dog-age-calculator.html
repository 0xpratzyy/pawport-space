Dog Age Calculator 🎂

The 7× rule is a myth, dogs sprint through their first two years, then age by size. Here's the real number.

Based on veterinary aging charts: ~15 human years in year one, ~9 in year two, then 4–7 per year by size. Every dog writes their own curve, this is the honest estimate.

Why the seven year rule is wrong

Multiplying by seven is tidy and almost never right. Dogs do most of their growing up in the first two years, then slow down, and the slowdown depends heavily on size. A one year old dog is not seven in human terms, it is closer to fifteen: sexually mature, physically grown, still making terrible decisions. By year two it is around twenty four. Only after that does the yearly increment settle into something steadier, and even then a small dog adds roughly four human years annually while a giant breed adds closer to seven.

That size split is the part the seven year rule erases completely, and it is the part that matters most. A Great Dane and a Chihuahua born on the same day are not the same age at any point after their first birthday, and by year eight they are living in genuinely different decades.

Life stages, and what each one asks of you

Puppy, birth to about a year. Everything happens at once: vaccinations, socialisation, teething, growth plates closing somewhere between twelve and eighteen months depending on size. Larger breeds stay puppies for longer, which is why a Labrador at fourteen months still behaves like a toddler while a Chihuahua of the same age has been an adult for months.

Adult, roughly one to seven. The steady stretch. Weight and dental care in these years decide a lot about how the senior years go, and they are the easiest years to let slide because nothing is obviously wrong.

Senior, from about seven for large breeds and ten for small. The greying starts, sleep gets longer, and the useful thing you can do is track rather than guess: weight every month, any new stiffness, changes in drinking or appetite. A pattern you can show a vet is worth far more than a memory of when it started.

What actually moves the number

  • Size, most of all. Big dogs age faster in a way researchers still find genuinely puzzling. It is the single strongest predictor.
  • Weight. Carrying extra weight is associated with a shorter life and earlier joint trouble. It is also the factor most within your control.
  • Dental health. Unglamorous and consistently underrated. Dental disease is common by three and does not stay confined to the mouth.
  • Neutering status and sex. Both shift average lifespans somewhat, though less than size or weight.
  • Breed specifics. Some lines carry known heart, hip or breathing risks that shape the later years more than any calculator can.

Common questions

How old is my dog in human years?

Enter their age and size above for the estimate. As a rough mental version: about fifteen human years for the first year, nine more for the second, then four to seven per year after that depending on size, with giant breeds at the top of that range.

Is one dog year really seven human years?

No. It underestimates the first two years badly and ignores size entirely. It survives because it is easy to remember, not because it is accurate.

When is a dog considered senior?

Around seven for large and giant breeds, closer to ten for small ones. Senior is a care category rather than a birthday: it is the point where twice yearly check ups and weight tracking start earning their keep.

Why do big dogs age faster?

Large dogs grow much faster in their first year and that accelerated growth appears to carry a cost later. It is an active area of research and no single explanation is settled, but the pattern itself is consistent and well documented.

How long do dogs live?

Very broadly, small breeds often reach fourteen to sixteen, medium and large breeds ten to thirteen, and giant breeds seven to ten. Individual dogs land outside these ranges in both directions, and weight and dental care shift the odds more than most owners expect.

Does my mixed breed follow its size or its breeds?

Use adult weight. A mix that settles at thirty kilos ages roughly like other thirty kilo dogs, whatever the DNA test says about its ancestry.
