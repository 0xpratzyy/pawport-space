Puppy's First Vet Visit

Book it for the first week home. It's mostly weighing, treats and paperwork, and it quietly decides whether your dog fears the vet for the next fifteen years.

The first vet visit has two jobs: a health baseline for a brand-new family member, and a first impression of the clinic itself. Handle the second one well and you get a dog who trots into the vet's office for life. Handle it badly and every future visit is a negotiation.

What to bring

  • Every paper you got from the breeder or shelter, vaccination card, deworming dates, microchip number
  • A stool sample (fresh-ish, in a bag), worms are near-universal in puppies and the test needs ammunition
  • A list of what they eat and how much (brand and grams, the portion talk starts here)
  • High-value treats, you, not the vet, are the treat dispenser today
  • A carrier or your arms, an under-vaccinated puppy shouldn't tour the waiting-room floor where sick dogs sat (same logic as early socialization)

What actually happens

StepWhat it covers
Weigh-inThe first data point of the growth curve, save it
Nose-to-tail examHeart, lungs, eyes, ears, mouth (bite alignment), belly, joints, skin and coat
Microchip checkScanned, and registration details verified, an unregistered chip is a serial number to nowhere
Parasite planDeworming schedule, flea/tick prevention suited to your area
VaccinesWhatever's due per the shot schedule, and the plan for the rest
The questionsYour list, see below

Questions worth asking while you're there

  1. What weight should this puppy be at six months, and when do we switch off puppy food?
  2. When is it safe to walk on public grass, and to meet unknown dogs?
  3. What's your emergency/out-of-hours arrangement? (Save the number now, not at 2am.)
  4. Spay/neuter: what timing do you recommend for this breed and size?
  5. Anything breed-specific to watch? (Flat-faced breeds and heat, big breeds and joints, small breeds and teeth.)

Make the clinic a happy place

Feed treats in the waiting room, during the exam, after the injection. Ask if you can drop by some week just to sit in the waiting room, get a treat from the receptionist and leave, "happy visits" are the single best vaccine against vet fear. And keep your own voice bored and cheerful during the injection; puppies read the room through you.

Afterwards

Mild sleepiness or a tender injection spot for a day is normal; facial swelling, vomiting or hives is not, call the clinic. Then file everything, weight, vaccines given, next due dates, somewhere you'll find it at booking time. A proper health record starts on day one, and this visit is its first page.

First visit, first stampWeight, vaccines and due-date reminders go in the health drawer; the brave face goes in the passport.
