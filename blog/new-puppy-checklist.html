New Puppy Checklist: Before Day One

The pet store will happily sell you forty things. You need about fifteen — and two of the most important items aren't things at all.

Puppy prep has two failure modes: buying everything (half unused by month two) and buying nothing (an emergency store run with a crying puppy in the car). This list is the middle path, sorted by when you'll actually need it.

Before pickup: the true essentials

ItemBuying advice
CrateSized for the adult dog, with a divider for now. Two doors beats one.
Bed + washable blanketsCheap for now — puppies chew and have accidents. The fancy bed is a 1st-birthday gift.
Food & water bowlsStainless steel or ceramic. Plastic scratches and harbours bacteria.
The breeder/shelter's foodSame brand for the first 2 weeks, then transition slowly. New home + new food = double trouble.
Collar or harness + ID tagPuppy-sized, adjustable. Order the tag before pickup, not after.
Light leash + long lineThe 10m line is the secret weapon for recall later.
Enzyme cleanerNon-negotiable. Regular cleaner hides accidents from you, not from the puppy's nose.
Chew toys (3–4 textures)Rubber, rope, something soft. Teething is coming whether you're ready or not.
Treats, tiny and softPea-sized. You'll use hundreds during potty training — size accordingly.
Puppy pads or a grass patchEven if you plan outdoor-only — for the blizzard/illness/3am contingency.

Can wait until week two+

  • Grooming kit — brush yes, everything else after the coat settles (see bathing by coat type)
  • Puzzle feeders — brilliant later, ignored by an 8-week-old
  • Fancy wardrobe — they'll outgrow it in six weeks anyway
  • Car harness/carrier — needed for the pickup drive only if it's long; a blanket-lined box and a passenger works for day one

The non-shopping checklist

  1. A vet appointment booked for the first week — the first visit is mostly paperwork, weighing and treats, and it sets up the vaccine schedule.
  2. House rules agreed before arrival. On the couch or not? Which door for potty? What's the cue word? Inconsistent humans are the number-one confusion for puppies.
  3. Puppy-proofing sweep — cables tucked, shoes elevated, houseplants checked (several common ones are toxic), bins lidded.
  4. The paperwork folder — vaccination card, microchip number, breeder contract, pedigree if any. You will be asked for these at daycare, boarding, training class and the groomer, forever.

Day one itself

Keep it quiet. No visitor parade, no puppy party — just the new humans, the new space, and a lot of naps. Show them the potty spot first, before the house. And take photos of absolutely everything: the first nap, the too-big collar, the first confused meeting with the stairs. Day-one photos are the ones you'll treasure hardest at the first birthday.
