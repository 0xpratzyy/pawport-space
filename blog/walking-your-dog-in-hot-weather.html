Walking Your Dog in Hot Weather

Dogs don't sweat, flat faces don't vent, and asphalt doesn't forgive. Here's when to walk, when to wait, and when to skip it entirely.

Heat is the most underestimated walk hazard because the danger is invisible: the air feels fine to a human in shoes and a t-shirt, while the dog is wearing a fur coat, barefoot on a griddle, cooling themselves through panting alone. A few rules cover almost everything.

Temperature guide

Air temperature is only the start — humidity, sun, and your dog's build move every line down.

Up to 19°C / 66°FWalk freelyWalk freely
20–23°C / 68–73°FFine, bring water on long walksKeep it moderate, watch the pant
24–27°C / 75–81°FShorter, shadier, slowerShort sniff-walks only, early or late
28–31°C / 82–88°FEarly morning / late evening onlyBathroom breaks only
32°C+ / 90°F+Skip it — enrichment indoors insteadSkip it, no exceptions

The 7-second pavement test

Press the back of your hand flat on the asphalt for seven seconds. If you can't hold it there comfortably, it will burn paw pads. Asphalt in full sun runs 20–30 degrees hotter than the air — a pleasant 25°C afternoon can mean 50°C+ pavement. Grass, dirt paths and shade change the math completely; timing (before 9am, after sunset) changes it even more.

Flat-faced breeds play by harder rules

Brachycephalic dogs — pugs, bulldogs, shih tzus, boxers, and friends — cool far less efficiently through their shortened airways. What's a comfortable walk for a beagle can be genuinely dangerous for a frenchie at the same temperature. If your dog snores in their sleep, treat every threshold in the table one row stricter. (This is also why walk duration guides split by breed group.)

Heatstroke: know the signs cold

Early: heavy relentless panting, thick drool, bright-red tongue and gums, seeking shade and refusing to move. Serious: wobbling, vomiting, glazed eyes, collapse. If you see the serious list — this is an emergency: move to shade, pour room-temperature (not ice) water over the body, especially belly and paws, and get to a vet immediately. Log the episode afterwards in the health record; dogs who've overheated once are more susceptible after.

Hot-day walk kit

  • Water + a collapsible bowl (offer every 15–20 minutes)
  • Route with shade and grass — parks over pavements
  • Harness over collar (nothing pressing on the throat while panting)
  • A plan B: sniff games, frozen treats and puzzle feeders burn energy indoors with zero heat risk

A hot summer doesn't mean no adventures — it means dawn patrols and golden-hour walks, which happen to be the prettiest stamps in the passport anyway.
