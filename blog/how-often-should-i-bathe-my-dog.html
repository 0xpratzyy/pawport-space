How Often Should I Bathe My Dog?

Less often than the shampoo bottle says, more often than your dog would vote for. The real answer depends on the coat, here's the breakdown.

Dogs don't need human-frequency bathing, their skin runs on a different oil economy, and stripping it too often causes exactly the itching and smell you were trying to wash away. The right schedule is set by coat type, lifestyle, and nose. Mostly nose.

Bathing frequency by coat type

CoatExamplesBathe every
Long, silkyShih Tzu, Yorkie, Maltese2–4 weeks
Curly / woolPoodle, Doodle mixes, Bichon3–4 weeks (with brushing between)
Double coatHusky, Golden, Shepherd, Corgi6–12 weeks, brush far more than you bathe
Short, smoothBeagle, Lab, Frenchie4–8 weeks
HairlessXolo, Chinese CrestedWeekly (their skin does more work)
Oily / water coatBasset, Retriever types4–6 weeks, degreasing shampoo occasionally

Lifestyle overrides the table: a beach dog, a mud enthusiast, or anyone who rolled in something victorious gets a bath today, schedule be damned. (Traveling? Bathe a few days before the trip, not the day of, see the travel checklist.)

Signs you're bathing too often

  • Dandruff or flaky skin that appeared after the routine got enthusiastic
  • Itching within a day or two of the bath
  • A coat that looks dull and feels dry instead of glossy
  • That "doggy smell" returning faster, over-stripped skin overproduces oil to compensate

Doing the bath right

  1. Brush first, always. Water sets tangles into mats like concrete, especially on long and curly coats.
  2. Lukewarm water, dogs run hotter than us and find our "nice hot shower" genuinely unpleasant.
  3. Dog shampoo only. Human shampoo (even baby shampoo) is the wrong pH for canine skin.
  4. Rinse longer than feels necessary. Residue is the number-one cause of post-bath itching.
  5. Dry properly. Towel hard, then air or cool-dryer to fully dry, damp undercoat is how hot spots start, especially on double coats. A shih tzu air-drying into a damp mop is also just funny-looking, which you should photograph.

Between baths

Brushing is the real hygiene program, it distributes oils, removes dirt, and lets you find lumps, ticks and skin changes early (log anything odd in the health record). Paw-rinses after muddy walks, a damp cloth for the face on flat-faced breeds, and ear checks after swimming cover the rest.

Bath day is also one of the most photogenic disasters in dog ownership. The mid-bath betrayal face, the post-bath zoomies, those are keepers.
