How Long Should I Walk My Dog?

Somewhere between 30 minutes and two hours a day — and where your dog lands depends on breed, age and the weather. Here's how to find your number.

"Enough exercise" is the most repeated and least specified advice in dog ownership. So let's specify it. These are working targets — start here, then read your actual dog, who did not read the chart.

Daily targets by breed group

Breed groupDaily totalFeels like
Working & herding (border collie, shepherd, husky)90–120+ minTwo long walks plus real play or a job
Sporting (labrador, golden, spaniel, pointer)60–90 minA long walk and a fetch session
Terriers & mid-energy mixes45–60 minTwo brisk walks with sniff time
Toy & companion (shih tzu, chihuahua, cavalier)30–45 minTwo or three short strolls
Flat-faced breeds (pug, bulldog, frenchie)20–40 minShort, slow, and never in heat

Split the total across at least two outings. Dogs are crepuscular by habit — a real morning walk and a real evening walk beat one giant slog, and your carpet will agree.

Adjust for age

  • Puppies: the common rule of thumb is about 5 minutes of structured walking per month of age, up to twice a day — roughly 15 minutes at three months, 20 at four. Free play on soft ground doesn't count against this; forced pavement miles do. Growth plates close between 12 and 18 months, and repetitive impact before then is the thing to avoid.
  • Adults: full chart values.
  • Seniors: keep the frequency, shrink the distance. Three 15-minute ambles maintain joints and routine better than one 45-minute march. Watch for stiffness the morning after — that's your signal you overshot the day before.

Adjust for weather

  • Above 25°C / 77°F: shift walks to early morning and late evening. Do the seven-second test — if the pavement is too hot for the back of your hand, it's too hot for paws.
  • Above 30°C / 86°F: short shade-and-sniff outings only. Flat-faced breeds overheat fastest; for them, treat anything warm as dangerous.
  • Cold: most dogs are fine well below freezing with movement; small, thin-coated and senior dogs need coats and shorter loops. Salt on the roads hurts pads — rinse paws after.
  • Rain: your dog cares far less than you do. Towel by the door, business as usual.

Signs you've got the number wrong

Under-walked looks like: destruction after you leave, barking at everything, pacing in the evening, pulling like a freight train the moment the leash clips on. Over-walked looks like: lagging behind on routes they used to lead, long recovery naps that swallow the whole day, paw licking, next-day stiffness. Both are your dog answering the question for you.

Quality beats mileage

A "sniffari" — a slow walk where the dog chooses the route and reads every message on every pole — tires a dog more than the same minutes at forced march. Scent work is cognitive load. If time is short, trade distance for sniffing and both of you win.

Track it, don't guess it

Most people dramatically overestimate how much they walk the dog in a normal week (workdays blur, weekends inflate the average). Recording walks for two ordinary weeks gives you the real number.
