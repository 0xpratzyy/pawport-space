Senior Dog Care: After Age Seven

Grey muzzles are earned. Here's what quietly changes, what's worth adjusting at home, and which "just old age" signs are actually treatable.

"Senior" isn't one age — a Great Dane is senior at six while a chihuahua is barely middle-aged at eight. But whenever it arrives, the playbook is the same: more frequent checkups, small home adjustments, and refusing to write treatable problems off as "just getting old."

When senior starts

SizeSenior fromCheckups
Small (<10 kg)~10–11 yearsEvery 6 months
Medium (10–25 kg)~8–9 yearsEvery 6 months
Large (25–40 kg)~7 yearsEvery 6 months
Giant (40+ kg)~6 yearsEvery 4–6 months

Twice-yearly vet visits with bloodwork matter because a dog year is roughly five to seven of ours — an annual checkup is like you seeing a doctor once every six years. Keep the results together; a tidy health record is what lets a vet spot a kidney value drifting over three visits instead of treating each number in isolation.

"Just old age" that's actually treatable

  • Slowing on walks / hesitating at stairs — usually arthritis, and modern pain management works well. Dogs hide pain heroically; stiffness after rest is the tell.
  • Fussiness about food — often dental pain, not pickiness. (Prevention lives in the tooth-brushing guide.)
  • Accidents indoors — could be kidneys, a UTI, or cognitive change; all manageable, none of them "naughtiness".
  • Night restlessness, staring at walls, getting "stuck" in corners — canine cognitive dysfunction is real and has treatment options, especially early.
  • Cloudy eyes — some of it is normal lens hardening; some is cataracts. Only an exam tells the difference.

Home adjustments that punch above their weight

  1. Traction. Rugs or yoga-mat runners on slippery floors are the single best arthritis accommodation.
  2. Ramps or steps to the sofa and car — jumping down does more damage than jumping up.
  3. A firmer, warmer bed away from drafts. Old joints hate cold floors.
  4. Shorter, more frequent walks beat one long haul — and sniffing time becomes the point, not the pace. Watch heat harder too: seniors sit a row stricter on every temperature threshold.
  5. Keep the brain fed. Puzzle feeders, new (gentle) routes, scent games — mental exercise slows cognitive decline measurably.

Weight becomes medicine

Every extra kilo lands on aging joints and organs. Senior metabolism drops, so yesterday's portions quietly become too much. Monthly weigh-ins and the rib check matter more now than at any other life stage.

And document these years generously. The grey-muzzle era is slower, sweeter and more photogenic than any puppy phase — it deserves more pages, not fewer.
