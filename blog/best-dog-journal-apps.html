Best Dog Journal Apps in 2026

Yes, we make one of these, and yes, we'll tell you when the others are the better pick. Here's how the field actually breaks down.

"Dog journal app" covers four different jobs that rarely live in one place: keeping memories, tracking health, logging walks, and coordinating care with other humans. Most apps pick one job. The honest way to choose is to decide which job you actually want done.

What actually separates these apps

1. Whether you'll still open it in month three

Every pet app graveyard is full of blank diaries. The apps that survive make entries take seconds (photo → done) and give you something back for the effort — a page, a streak, a book you'd show someone. Judge any journal app by how it feels to flip back through, because that's the product you're really buying.

2. Where your data lives

Photos of your dog plus vet records plus your location history is a surprisingly sensitive bundle. Prefer apps that store on-device or in your own cloud account, and read the deletion policy. If it's free with no business model in sight, the business model is you.

3. Whether health tracking is structured

A note that says "rabies done ✓" is not a vaccine record. Look for typed entries with dates and due-date reminders, and a way to export the whole thing — the seven-item checklist we use for evaluating health features applies to apps too.

4. Multi-pet and multi-human support

Two dogs and two humans is the median dog household. If that's you, shared access matters more than any other feature — an immaculate journal only one person can update becomes one person's chore.

Where Pawport fits

The pitch: your dog's life as an official little passport. Walks get stamped with the route and weather, baths and birthdays become pages, on-device photo scanning suggests stamps from pictures you already took, and a proper health drawer (vaccines with reminders, weight curve, exportable PDF) lives behind the fun. Everything stays in your own iCloud — no accounts, no ads, no analytics. The core app is free; an optional subscription adds plush passport covers, monthly AI-portrait credits and extra dogs.

Where it isn't for you: it's iPhone-only right now, and it's built for memory-keeping first — if your main need is coordinating a dog-walker rota or formal training plans, one of the logistics or training apps will serve you better. We'd rather you pick the right tool than churn out of ours.

How to choose in five minutes

  1. Name the job (memories / health / logistics / training).
  2. Install the one app that leads on that job — not three.
  3. Add today's entry, then set a reminder for a week out: "still using it?"
  4. If yes, import your backlog. If no, delete guilt-free and try the runner-up.
